# M&M Launcher Turret: Design Brief

> **Change notice (after attempt 1 started):** the encoder magnet goes on the motor's **rear** shaft, through the end-cap access hole. See the BLDC row. Any design that puts the magnet on the front shaft or flywheel must be updated, and reviewers should flag it.
>
> **Change notice 2:** the MotorGo Axis USB-C location is now confirmed. It is a vertical-mount port (see the MotorGo row), so the cable exits perpendicular to the board face. Designs must provide a plug cutout and cable exit path that match it, and reviewers should check it.
>
> **Change notice 4:** an exact MotorGo Axis model is now in `reference/mg_axis.step` / `.stl`. Use it for the placeholder, the standoff height (header pins reach 7.25 mm below the board), and the USB-C cutout position.
>
> **Change notice 3:** servo horn (round 8-hole disc, recess plus drill-through-as-template) and servo controller (58 x 42 PCB, drill-to-fit bosses plus a locating pocket) are now specified in the component table.

A desktop turret that holds a hopper of standard M&Ms (plain, about 13 mm across and 7 mm thick, lentil shaped), feeds them one at a time into a spinning flywheel, and launches them at an adjustable speed. Pan/tilt aiming. It's controlled from a browser over WebUSB, and the browser shows the CAD moving live, so moving parts must be separate objects with clean pivots.

## Hard constraints
- **Material:** all structure is PLA. No TPU. The wheel grip surface is hockey tape or similar wrapped on a PLA rim, so leave a tape land on the rim (flat, about 12–15 mm wide). Make wheel-to-hood compression adjustable (slots or shims), because grip and pressure will be tuned by hand.
- **Printers:** Bambu Lab H2D (single-nozzle build volume 325 x 320 x 325 mm) and P1S (256 x 256 x 256 mm). Prefer every printed part to fit the P1S. Parts must print without heavy supports: a flat face on the bed and overhangs of 45° or less, or bridges.
- **Assembly:** one person with a DeWalt hand drill and a full bit set, superglue, hot glue, M3 machine screws (use heat-set inserts only if needed; prefer nut traps or self-tapping M3 into 2.5 mm pilot holes), and self-tapping M3 screws. No other fasteners or bearings are assumed. If a 608 bearing or a lazy-susan bearing would help a lot, it may be listed as optional, but the design must work without it.
- **Cables:** every cable has a planned path: motor phase leads (3 x Ø4 bullet leads, 80 mm), JST 5-pin encoder cables, the servo bus daisy chain, 12 V power, and a USB-C cable from the MotorGo Axis out to a laptop. No visible loose wires. Cables crossing the pan axis need a defined pass-through and slack loop for ±90° pan or more.
- **Power:** a DeWalt 20V battery slides onto the DeWalt adapter, which feeds the 12 V buck converter, which feeds the MotorGo Axis (6–17 V input) and the servo controller board. The battery must be removable without tools. The buck converter needs airflow around its fins.

## Functional requirements
1. The hopper holds at least 60 M&Ms and has a lid. It feeds reliably with no bridging: steep walls (≥ 50°), plus an agitating, indexed feeder (for example a pocketed rotary disk driven by servo #3) that releases exactly one M&M per fire command.
2. The feed path delivers the M&M into the wheel/hood nip at a defined point. A single PLA flywheel (rim about Ø60–80 mm with a tape land) is direct-driven by the 3674 on the 5 mm D-shaft (M3 set screw and nut trap, or a clamping hub). It needs a curved hood or backplate with an adjustable gap of about 9–12 mm before tape, and a muzzle that sets the exit direction. Target wheel speed is 3,000–6,000 rpm.
3. The wheel must be fully guarded except for the feed entry and the muzzle. Nothing should be able to fly off sideways.
4. Pan servo (≥ ±90°) and tilt servo (about 0 to +45° elevation), with the load path carried by printed structure, not the servo spline alone, wherever practical.
5. The encoder is mounted rigidly and coaxially with the motor shaft and is serviceable.
6. All electronics are mounted with screws and enclosed or tidily exposed, with access to the USB-C, servo controller ports, power switch, and battery. There is an air path for the buck converter and the MotorGo.
7. Stable: it doesn't tip at full tilt or pan, and it stays put when firing.

## Aesthetic goals
Looks designed, not slapped together: consistent fillets and chamfers, a coherent form language, aligned parting lines, hidden fasteners where practical, concealed cable channels, and visual unity between the base, turret, and launcher. Render with a restrained palette: printed parts in two coordinated PLA colors, and purchased parts in realistic neutral colors.

## Deliverables (all under `mm-launch-hw/`)
- `blender/build_assembly.py`: a **parametric** Blender Python script (Blender 5.1, run headless with `/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b --python <script>`) that builds the whole assembly from named parameters at the top. Use collections `Printed`, `Purchased`, and `Fasteners`. Name objects `PRT_<part>` / `BUY_<part>`. Moving links get empties as pivots: `PIV_pan`, `PIV_tilt`, `PIV_flywheel`, `PIV_feeder`, parented so that rotating an empty moves its subtree. Use bmesh/modifiers (booleans, bevels) so the printed parts are manifold solids.
- `blender/mm_launcher.blend`: the saved assembly.
- `stl/<part>.stl`: one file per printed part, in its print orientation, in mm.
- `blender/checks.py`: an automated report written to `renders/attempt-N/checks.json` covering every printed part's manifold status (non-manifold edge count), bounding box against the P1S and H2D volumes, pairwise interference between parts (BVH overlap), and estimated print time/filament (volume-based).
- `renders/attempt-N/`: PNG renders at 1600 px or more: iso, front, side, top, rear (cable side), exploded view, a section through the launcher (wheel, hood, feed), and pan at -90/+90 with tilt at 0/45.
- `BOM_AND_ASSEMBLY.md`: parts list, print settings per part (orientation, supports yes/no, infill), fastener list, and numbered assembly steps for one person.
