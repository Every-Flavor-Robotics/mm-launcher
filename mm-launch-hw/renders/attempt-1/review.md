# Independent review: M&M launcher turret, attempt 1

Reviewer re-ran `checks.py` (output: `review/checks_rerun.json`, same result as the designer's: 35 printed objects manifold, all fit the P1S, 0 volumetric interferences among PRT/BUY at 9 poses, tip angle >= 44.1 deg). The designer's checks **leave out fasteners and cables**, and they only test the nominal tilt range. My own measurements (`review/measurements.json`, scripts run on a copy of the .blend) and the extra renders in `review/` cover those gaps.

## Scores

| Metric | Score | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Functionality | **5 / 10** | Base, pan/tilt, power, USB and stability are solid. The core launch function is not: the M&M lands outside the hood, often on a part of the wheel whose tangent points back up the chute, and the nip never squeezes a flat M&M. |
| Manufacturability | **6 / 10** | Everything fits the P1S with no supports and sensible PLA fits. As drawn, though, the feeder disc collides with a screw head, the horn adapters can't be assembled in the stated order, and a tape or shim change means taking the launcher out of the yoke. |
| Aesthetics | **5 / 10** | The base is clean and the two-colour palette works. The launcher pod has a sweep-cut V-notch, an exposed motor and lead duct with yellow leads showing, a boxed servo hanging outboard, and form languages that don't match. |

## Findings, most impactful first

1. **Blocker (functionality): the feed delivers the M&M to the wrong part of the wheel.** At tilt 0 the M&M lands 43 to 76 deg behind TDC, outside the 26 deg hood lip. From a contact at 76 deg, and at every contact at tilt 45 (about 78 deg, against the rear wall), the tape's tangent points straight back up the chute toward the feeder plate (`review/nip_section_tilt0.png`, `review/nip_section_tilt45.png`, red rods). The hood's concentric arc is only 11 mm long. Expect blow-back, cracked shells, and exit speed that changes with tilt. *Fix:* move the drop point (or the tilt axis T) about 25 mm rearward and add a ramped chute floor that delivers the M&M tangentially at 20 to 30 deg behind TDC at every tilt. Extend the hood's concentric section to about 60 to 70 deg behind TDC with a 5 to 8 deg converging lead-in, so the first tape contact always happens under the hood.
2. **Major (functionality): the nip never squeezes the M&M.** A flat M&M is about 7 mm. Bare-rim gaps of 8.9/10.5/12.1 mm minus about 0.5 mm of tape leave 1.4 to 4.6 mm of slack, and the +/-1.6 mm slots prevent anything tighter. *Fix:* lengthen the hood slots to +/-4 mm and replace the shims with two M3 jack screws through the guard roof (plus lock nuts). That gives continuous adjustment from about 6.0 to 12 mm, so you can tune 0.5 to 1.5 mm of compression.
3. **Blocker (manufacturability): the arm-cap screw R-1 head sits under the feeder disc.** The screw at (47, -19.6) is 32.4 mm from the feeder axis, inside the Ø100 disc and the hopper. Its head is 2.5 mm tall; the disc has 0.4 mm clearance (`review/feeder_disc_vs_cap_screw.png`). *Fix:* counterbore Ø6.5 x 3.2 mm in the plate top at both right-cap holes and use low-head or countersunk M3 x 40, or move CAP_SCREW_Y outside r = 52 of the feeder axis.
4. **Major (manufacturability): the horn adapter can't be assembled as written.** Screws through the horn holes would have to be driven from the servo side, which is inaccessible once the horn is on the spline. Meanwhile the horn's centre screw is trapped under a closed 12 AF hex key (`review/tilt_horn_adapter_closeup.png`). Screwing in from the hex side instead puts M2 heads at r = 7, which collides with the hex flanks (r 6 to 6.93). *Fix:* add a Ø6.5 through-hole on axis through the key, fix horn to adapter on the bench (countersunk M2 from the horn side), then fit the assembly to the spline and drive the centre screw through the hole. Update steps 5, 21, 22 and the "Horn adapters" section.
5. **Major (functionality): the tilt range has no margin.** At 45 deg there is 0.21 to 0.37 mm to the hopper and feeder plate; they touch at 46 deg and overlap at 47.5 deg. PLA tolerance, plate sag or servo overshoot will cause a crash. *Fix:* extend `sweep_cutters` to 50 deg, add a printed hard stop on the right arm at 46 deg, and clamp the servo to 0 to 45 deg in firmware.
6. **Major (manufacturability/functionality): routine service needs a full launcher teardown.** The cover carries the right journal, which sits inside the right arm bore, so the cover can't come off in place. Changing tape, changing shims or re-tightening the flywheel set screw means removing the feeder plate (with the hopper and feeder servo), both caps and the bonnet, then lifting the pod out: about 15 screws. *Fix:* put the right journal on the body (or on a separate stub screwed through the cover), and add a removable screwed hatch over the hood bay or wheel top, so tape and shims are reachable with the pod installed.
7. **Major (functionality): nothing controls how the magnet is centred.** The Ø6 magnet is glued onto the end of a Ø5 shaft through a Ø8 hole "with a printed or paper sleeve". The sensor side is coaxial by construction (pegs and IC centred to 0.1 mm), but the magnet's own centring error can easily exceed the 0.3 mm requirement. *Fix:* add `encoder_jig.stl`, a Ø36.1 plug for the cradle bore with a Ø6.1 x 2 mm pocket on axis that holds the magnet on the shaft while the glue cures. List it in the assembly steps.
8. **Minor (functionality): the feeder baffle shears M&Ms standing on edge.** A Ø15.5 pocket 8.4 mm deep lets an M&M stand on edge (13 mm), so it sticks out about 4.6 mm into a baffle with a vertical radial leading face and 1.0 mm clearance. The 35 kg-cm servo will crush it or stall. Also, six pockets at 60 deg against a 240 deg servo needs an oscillating 0/60 deg index, which isn't documented. *Fix:* give the baffle's leading edge a 30 deg ramp (or a flexible flap) and document the oscillating index in the BOM.
9. **Minor (functionality): the outlet is partly blocked at high tilt.** At 40 to 45 deg the front 1 to 3 mm of the Ø16 outlet lands on the guard top 1 mm below the plate. This contradicts the log's claim that the column clears at both extremes, and creates a shear point if you tilt mid-drop. *Fix:* widen the fan's front edge by 10 deg and lock out tilt moves while a feed is in progress.
10. **Minor (functionality): the motor leads' bullet joints land at the moving bend.** Path length from the lead block to the bonnet is about 71 mm against 80 mm leads, so the three rigid bullet joints end up at the trunnion exit and 90 deg bend in a 6 mm-deep bonnet cavity (`review/bonnet_cable_turn_closeup.png`). *Fix:* plan the joints inside the straight trunnion bore (staggered, cut the leads to about 55 mm) or solder the extensions, and deepen the bonnet to 10 mm with a 6 mm bend radius at the exit.
11. **Minor (functionality): the trunnion end face rubs the bonnet flange** with 0.00 mm clearance at every tilt. *Fix:* shorten the journal by 0.5 mm, or add a 0.5 mm relief ring in the bonnet face.
12. **Minor (functionality): the cable plan has no UART link.** Nothing routes the MotorGo-to-servo-controller UART (the brief asks for it), about 200 mm across the base. *Fix:* add the route and a clip along the floor-plate boss row.
13. **Minor (manufacturability): purchased-part fit assumptions.** The DeWalt adapter is fixed with M3 x 25 self-tappers into corner holes of unknown size, and the battery bay has only 1.0 mm side clearance against a nominal envelope. *Fix:* use through-bolts with nuts, or a printed strap cradle, and open the bay to 2.0 mm per side with a 1 mm lead-in chamfer.
14. **Minor (manufacturability): hard-to-reach screws.** The trunnion screws sit at the bottom of a 102 mm, Ø20 bore and need an extension of at least 110 mm. The encoder-cap screw at y = 5.5 sits 4 mm in front of the left arm and is only reachable at tilt 45. *Fix:* list the extension in the BOM or move the trunnion screws outside the bore onto a flange, and add a "tilt to 45 to service the encoder" note.
15. **Minor (manufacturability): the model and checks hide clashes.** The motor-face screws are modelled reversed (their shanks sit inside the flywheel), and `checks.py` skips fasteners and cables, which is why item 3 and the reversed motor screws weren't caught. *Fix:* set the direction to (-1, 0, 0) for FST_motor and add FST/CAB-vs-moving-part checks across the tilt sweep, including at 47.5 deg.
16. **Major (aesthetics): the launcher pod looks unfinished.** It has the sweep-cut V-notch, an exposed motor can and lead duct with yellow phase leads visible at the duct-to-trunnion gap (`front.png`, `iso_front_left.png`), the Plink PCB edge showing past the encoder cap (`pan+90_tilt45.png`), and a boxed tilt servo hanging 50 mm outboard of the right arm. *Fix:* restyle the notch as one deliberate 45 deg chamfer that runs the full pod width. Add a terracotta shroud from the guard over the motor, duct and encoder cap, sharing the guard's outline, and a matching cover on the tilt servo that continues the right-arm profile.
17. **Minor (aesthetics): inconsistent detailing.** Edge breaks vary from 0.6 to 1.5 mm and corner radii from 4 to 16 mm. The tops of the feeder plate, arm feet and cover all show exposed screw heads. The muzzle is a plain rectangular hole showing the 3.2 mm slot above the hood (`review/muzzle_front.png`). *Fix:* standardise on a 1.0 mm edge break and 6 mm corner radius, counterbore the visible heads, and give the muzzle a short flared lip that closes the slot.
18. **Minor (functionality): flywheel retention.** Shaft engagement is 8.5 mm with a single set screw into a PLA nut trap at 6,000 rpm, and there's no balance provision. The motor can is fully enclosed in a PLA sleeve. *Fix:* use a split clamping hub (M3 pinch bolt) with 12 mm engagement and add an opposing M3 balance-screw boss. Check can temperature on the first runs.

## What is good
- Low centre of gravity, with tip angle >= 44 deg at every pose. Pan +/-120 deg, with the cable crossing on-axis through the hub bore and a U loop below.
- The cable path across the tilt axis goes through the trunnion bore, so the cables only twist.
- The encoder sensor is coaxial by construction (the spigot shares the Ø36.3 motor bore, the pegs match the measured 16 mm hole square, and the IC is centred), with a 1.0 mm gap.
- The vertical USB-C plugs straight through the partition into a niche and exits the rear, with a zip-tie anchor.
- The buck converter sits by low and high louvres, and the controller switch and terminal are reachable through the right-wall notch.
- Every part fits the P1S with no supports. Fits are sensible: 0.2 mm journals, 0.15 mm hex, 0.3 mm servo pockets.
